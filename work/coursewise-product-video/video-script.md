# Coursewise Product Story Video Script

Total video length: 3:45

## Slide 1 — Coursewise (0:00–0:20)

Hi everyone. For this project, I designed Coursewise, a mobile competitor to Canvas Student. Coursewise helps students understand their grades and feedback, keep track of deadlines, and actually plan when to do their work. I wanted the mobile experience to be useful for more than just checking information.

## Slide 2 — The user need (0:20–0:50)

I started by interviewing four students about how they currently use Canvas. Most of them said they mainly open the mobile app to check grades and deadlines. When they need to read detailed feedback or act on it, they usually move back to their laptop. They also use separate tools like Google Calendar or a to-do list because Canvas does not give them enough planning support.

## Slide 3 — What Canvas leaves unfinished (0:50–1:18)

That research showed me that the problem was bigger than confusing navigation. Important pieces of the student experience felt disconnected. A grade could appear without showing what it means for the overall course. Feedback was difficult to locate. And seeing a deadline did not help students figure out when they should start working. This became the main opportunity for Coursewise.

## Slide 4 — Understanding a grade (1:18–1:50)

The first core flow begins when a student receives a new grade. On the Today page, Coursewise clearly shows the assignment and the course it belongs to. When the student opens it, they can see the score and how it changed their overall course grade. In the Grades section, the score breakdown, AI summary, and instructor feedback are kept together, so students do not have to search through the original assignment page.

## Slide 5 — Turning a deadline into a plan (1:50–2:25)

The second core flow helps students plan their work. When an assignment is due soon, Coursewise suggests realistic work sessions before the deadline. Students can use that suggestion or choose their own date, start time, duration, and calendar. After they save it, the session appears in the Plan tab next to the original deadline. The labels and visual styles help students tell the difference between something that is due and time they scheduled for themselves.

## Slide 6 — Reasons and confirmation (2:25–2:57)

I also wanted the scheduling feature to feel transparent. Coursewise explains why it recommends a particular time. For example, it might say that the student has two open hours before tomorrow’s deadline. After saving, the confirmation repeats the task, time, duration, and calendar destination. Students can then open the session in Plan, edit it, or remove it without deleting the original assignment deadline.

## Slide 7 — Testing and iteration (2:57–3:27)

I tested the prototype with four students. Overall, ninety percent of the tasks were completed without help. But the testing still showed me where the design needed improvement. Some students had trouble finding feedback. Others were unsure whether a schedule had actually been saved, or could not immediately distinguish deadlines from planned work. I used those findings to make the feedback path clearer and strengthen the schedule confirmation and Plan page.

## Slide 8 — Final product (3:27–3:45)

The final result is a mobile academic companion that connects grades, feedback, deadlines, and planning. Coursewise addresses the user need by helping students understand what happened and decide what to do next. The full interactive prototype is available at the link shown here.
